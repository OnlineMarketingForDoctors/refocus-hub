Children & young people psychiatric assessments

Understanding what is driving the difficulties

Children and young people can struggle with anxiety, low mood, obsessive thoughts, trauma, tics or school refusal, and the reasons are not always obvious. At Refocus, our consultant child and adolescent psychiatrists take time to understand what is driving the difficulties and how best to support recovery.

Our assessments follow NICE guidance and recognised NHS standards, with clear verbal feedback and written recommendations so families know exactly what happens next.

Is it right for my child?

Is a psychiatric assessment right for my child?

A psychiatric assessment may be helpful if your child or young person is experiencing:

  • Ongoing anxiety or panic symptoms
  • Obsessive thoughts or compulsive behaviours (OCD)
  • Low mood or emotional dysregulation
  • Trauma-related symptoms or PTSD
  • Tics or Tourette’s syndrome
  • Selective mutism or school refusal
  • Emotional and behavioural difficulties at home or school

Some children present with clear mental-health symptoms, while others show distress through behaviour, avoidance, or physical complaints. A specialist psychiatric assessment helps us understand what is driving the difficulties and how best to support recovery.

Why it matters

Why a comprehensive psychiatric assessment matters

Without a full understanding of a child’s developmental and emotional history:

  • Symptoms may be treated in isolation rather than in context
  • Underlying neurodevelopmental differences may be missed
  • Interventions may be less effective or poorly tolerated

Our assessments focus on:

  • Mapping symptoms across development
  • Identifying triggers, protective factors, and patterns over time
  • Recommendations that are individualised, realistic, and clinically meaningful

Our approach

Our Refocus psychiatric assessment approach

All our generic psychiatric assessments:

  • NICE & NHS standards

    Follow NICE guidance and recognised NHS standards

  • Consultant-led

    Are conducted by consultant child & adolescent psychiatrists

  • Evidence-based screening

    Use structured, evidence-based screening tools

  • Team review

    Are discussed within a clinical team where appropriate

Every Refocus psychiatric assessment includes:

  • A developmental history appointment with parents/carers
  • Review of emotional, behavioural, social, and educational functioning
  • Evidence-based questionnaires completed by parents/carers and the young person (where age-appropriate)
  • A one-to-one mental state examination with the child or young person
  • Clear formulation linking symptoms, development, and environment

Screening tools

Evidence-based screening tools we use

These tools support clinical judgement and help track patterns over time.

SDQStrengths and Difficulties Questionnaire

Completed by parents/carers and, where appropriate, young people.

It helps screen for:

  • Emotional symptoms
  • Conduct difficulties
  • Hyperactivity/inattention
  • Peer relationship challenges
  • Prosocial strengths
RCADSRevised Children’s Anxiety and Depression Scale

Completed by parents and young people.

It provides structured insight into:

  • Anxiety disorders (including panic and social anxiety)
  • Depressive symptoms
  • Severity and functional impact

How do our general psychiatry assessments work?

At Refocus, we offer comprehensive child and adolescent mental health (CAMHS) psychiatric assessments, delivered exclusively by experienced consultant child and adolescent psychiatrists. Our assessments follow NICE and NHS standards and high-quality, evidence-based assessment.

During the appointment, the psychiatrist will take a developmental, emotional, and mental health history, explore current concerns in depth, and consider strengths, vulnerabilities, and protective factors.

Depending on the reason for referral, pre-assessment questionnaires will be requested from:

  • Parents or carers
  • The young person themselves (age-appropriate)

School information is not routinely required at the pre-assessment stage, but may be requested later if clinically indicated.

Following the assessment, families receive clear verbal feedback, diagnostic impressions where appropriate, and written recommendations to guide next steps and support.

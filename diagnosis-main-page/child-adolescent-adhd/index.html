Child & adolescent ADHD

Understanding your child’s brain, not just labelling it

Diagnosing ADHD in children and young people can be complex. Attention, behaviour, learning, emotional regulation, sleep, anxiety, and sensory differences often overlap with other neurodevelopmental or mental-health conditions. At Refocus, our goal is not simply to label difficulties, but to deeply understand your child’s brain, development, and lived experience, so we can guide meaningful, personalised support.

All Refocus assessments are NHS-recognised and fully compliant with NICE guidelines and the ADHD Assessment Quality Assurance Standard for Children and Teenagers (CAAQAS), ensuring gold-standard, evidence-based diagnostic practice.

Is it ADHD?

Is an ADHD assessment right for my child?

ADHD can look very different from one child to another:

  • Visibly active

    Some children are visibly hyperactive and impulsive

  • Quietly struggling

    Others appear calm but struggle with focus, organisation, emotional regulation, or mental fatigue

  • Masking at school

    Many children (especially girls) mask their difficulties at school and only “fall apart” at home

Symptoms commonly overlap with:

  • Autism spectrum differences
  • Anxiety, low mood, trauma or attachment difficulties
  • Sleep disorders, sensory processing differences, or medical conditions
  • Dyslexia and learning differences

If you’ve been told “something isn’t quite right” but answers feel unclear or inconsistent, a specialist, multidisciplinary assessment can bring clarity and reassurance.

Why it matters

Why early and accurate diagnosis matters

Children with undiagnosed ADHD are often misunderstood.

They may be labelled as:

  • “Naughty”, “lazy”, “not trying hard enough”
  • “Bright but not reaching potential”
  • “Too emotional” or “too sensitive”

Over time, repeated criticism can deeply impact:

  • Self-esteem and confidence
  • Emotional wellbeing
  • School engagement and peer relationships

An early, thoughtful diagnosis helps adults respond differently, reduce blame, and support your child in ways that genuinely work for their brain.

The gold standard

What is the Young DIVA-5?

The Young DIVA-5 is a gold-standard, NICE-aligned diagnostic interview for ADHD in children and adolescents.

It:

  • Maps symptoms directly to DSM-5 ADHD criteria
  • Explores how symptoms present across settings (home, school, social life)
  • Considers developmental history, age-related changes, and functional impact
  • Helps differentiate ADHD from other conditions that can look similar

Using the Young DIVA-5 ensures the assessment is structured, consistent, and clinically robust, while still allowing space for your child’s individual story.

qEEG Brain MappingWhen indicated

qEEG brain mapping is available alongside your assessment at the reduced rate of £200.

qEEG is not required for diagnosis, but in complex cases it can:

  • Add neurobiological insight when questionnaires give mixed signals
  • Support understanding of attention, regulation, and processing patterns
  • Help guide personalised recommendations and future interventions

We are transparent about when qEEG is helpful and when it is not, and it is never used in isolation.

Step by step

The assessment process

  1. Once a deposit is paid, families receive secure access to our digital patient portal via electronic help record (ETR).

    Through ETR, we send:

    • Parent and school questionnaires
    • Developmental and mental-health screening tools
    • Age-appropriate child/young person forms

    Our clinicians carefully review all information before the appointment so your time together is focused, efficient, and meaningful.

  2. You and your child meet with a senior clinician experienced in ADHD and neurodevelopmental conditions.

    The session includes the Young DIVA-5 interview, alongside:

    • Early development and milestones
    • School and learning experiences
    • Emotional wellbeing and relationships
    • Strengths, interests, and coping strategies

    We also explore co-occurring conditions, as ADHD rarely exists in isolation. Where possible, we will share our clinical impression during the appointment and discuss next steps together.

  3. You will receive a comprehensive, clear, and practical report, including:

    • Diagnostic outcome (where criteria are met)
    • Differential diagnoses considered
    • Individualised recommendations

    We understand families often wish to return to the NHS. We will support you in understanding shared-care options, while being honest about local GP variability.

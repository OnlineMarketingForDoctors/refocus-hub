Adult autism

How your mind works, and how you have adapted

At Refocus, our goal is not simply to identify autism, but to understand how your mind works, how you have adapted over a lifetime, and what support will genuinely help.

Every assessment is Consultant-led and follows NICE guideline CG142 on autism diagnosis in adults, using the structured tools NICE recommends — the ADI-R developmental interview and the ADOS-2 observation.

Is it autism?

Is an autism assessment right for me?

Autism in adulthood rarely looks the way people expect. Some adults recognise themselves the moment they read about it. Others have spent decades appearing to cope, while privately managing:

  • Exhaustion after ordinary social contact
  • Sensory overload — noise, light, texture, crowds
  • A real need for routine, and genuine distress when it breaks
  • Unwritten social rules that others seem to absorb effortlessly
  • Deep, focused interests that have always felt central

Many adults — particularly women, and those given a diagnosis of anxiety or depression first — develop sophisticated masking strategies, and only seek an assessment after years of burnout or feeling persistently misunderstood.

Autistic traits are commonly mistaken for:

  • Anxiety and chronic stress
  • Low mood or depression
  • ADHD and executive function difficulties
  • Trauma or attachment difficulties
  • Sleep difficulties and emotional dysregulation
  • Social anxiety or avoidant traits

If you have spent years feeling that “something doesn’t quite fit”, or earlier explanations have never fully made sense, a specialist adult autism assessment can offer clarity and direction.

Why it matters

Why accurate diagnosis matters

Adults who are autistic without knowing it often absorb years of self-blame for finding things harder than everyone else appeared to.

Over time, that can affect:

  • Confidence and self-esteem
  • Relationships and family life
  • Work, study and career progression
  • Autistic burnout and exhaustion
  • Emotional wellbeing and resilience

A careful, evidence-based assessment can:

  • Reduce shame
  • Deepen self-understanding
  • Open the door to adjustments and support that actually fit

For many people, an autism assessment is not about “getting a label.” It is about finally understanding why life has taken so much more effort than it seemed to for everyone else.

Our approach

Our approach at Refocus: beyond questionnaires

Autism is a lifelong difference in how the brain processes social information, sensory input and change — which is why a meaningful assessment needs far more than a screening questionnaire. At Refocus, we combine the structured tools NICE recommends with a holistic understanding of the brain, mental health and lived experience.

  • Consultant Psychiatrist

    Assessment led by an experienced Consultant Psychiatrist trained in ADOS-2 and ADI-R — the tools NICE recommends for diagnosing autism.

  • Developmental History

  • Structured ADI-R Interview

  • Pre-assessment Questionnaires

  • Screening for co-occurring conditions

    Anxiety, mood difficulties, ADHD traits, autistic burnout and emotional dysregulation.

The gold standard

What are the ADOS-2 and ADI-R?

The ADOS-2 (Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule) and the ADI-R (Autism Diagnostic Interview–Revised) are the structured assessment tools NICE recommends for diagnosing autism.

Together they:

  • Observe social communication and interaction directly, rather than relying on self-report
  • Explore developmental history from early childhood through to the present
  • Examine patterns of behaviour, focused interests and sensory experiences
  • Help distinguish autism from conditions that can present similarly

Using the ADOS-2 and ADI-R together makes the assessment structured, clinically robust and evidence-based, while still leaving room for your own story and lived experience.

Before you meet us

Pre-assessment questionnaires

Before your appointment, you will complete a series of evidence-based screening tools through our secure patient portal. These may include:

  • AQ-10Autism Spectrum Quotient, 10-item
  • WellbeingMood, anxiety and wellbeing screening tools
  • Daily livingSensory and daily-living questionnaires
  • History formsAdditional developmental and psychosocial history forms

Informant input — central to an autism assessment

The ADI-R is an informant interview, so wherever possible we invite input from someone who knew you as a child — a parent, older sibling or close family member — alongside someone who knows you well now. Where no childhood informant is available, we adapt the assessment and tell you openly how that shapes it. Informant input helps us:

  • Build a fuller, more balanced picture than self-report alone can give
  • Understand how traits were present in early development
  • Meet the diagnostic standard, which requires evidence of early developmental onset

At Refocus we never rely on self-report alone. We bring several perspectives together so your assessment is clinically robust, evidence-based, and a true reflection of how your mind works.

Beyond the interview

Advanced tools we may include

Depending on your presentation and clinical complexity, we may also recommend additional neuroscience-informed assessments.

qEEG Brain Mapping (when clinically indicated)

qEEG brain mapping is available alongside your assessment at the reduced rate of £200.

qEEG plays no part in diagnosing autism, but in selected cases it can:

  • Give additional insight into how your brain regulates attention and arousal
  • Help make sense of sensory sensitivity, overload and fatigue
  • Guide personalised recommendations and any future neuroscience-led support

NICE is explicit that no brain scan, blood test or genetic test diagnoses autism. We are equally clear about when qEEG adds something useful and when it does not — and it is never used to make a diagnosis.

Step by step

The assessment process

  1. Once a deposit is paid, you receive secure access to our electronic health record patient portal.

    You will receive:

    • Pre-assessment questionnaires
    • Informant forms
    • Developmental and mental-health screening tools
    • Consent and background forms

    Our clinicians review everything before your appointment, so the session itself stays focused and meaningful.

    • You will meet a senior Consultant Psychiatrist trained in ADOS-2 and ADI-R
    • The ADOS-2 observation is carried out by a highly specialised ADOS-trained practitioner

    The session explores:

    • Early development, childhood and education
    • Social communication and relationships
    • Sensory experiences and environment
    • Routines, focused interests, and how change feels
    • Masking, coping strategies, and what they cost
    • Mental health and any coexisting conditions

    All clinical information is brought together in a multidisciplinary team discussion. Autism rarely exists in isolation — it frequently co-occurs with ADHD — which is why we take time to understand the wider picture rather than traits in isolation.

    Where appropriate, we will share our clinical impression during the appointment and discuss next steps together.

  3. You will receive a comprehensive, clear and practical report including:

    • Diagnostic outcome (where criteria are met)
    • Differential diagnoses considered
    • A profile of your strengths, needs and sensory world
    • Personalised recommendations for workplace or educational adjustments, psychological support and coaching, sensory and environmental strategies, and emotional wellbeing and burnout recovery
    • Guidance on any coexisting conditions identified, and how to take them forward

    Support after an autism diagnosis is about understanding, adjustments and any coexisting conditions — not about treating autism itself. Your report is written to be used: with employers, universities, your GP and wider support services.
